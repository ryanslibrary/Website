Plug-In Solar Is Officially Legal in the UK from 27 August 2026

📅 In force 27 August 2026 ⏱ 4 min read 📌 800W cap per kit

From 27 August 2026, a fully certified "plug-in" solar panel can legally connect straight into a normal 13A wall socket — no roof, no scaffolding, and no MCS-certified rooftop installer required. It's a genuinely new option for the millions of UK households who've never been able to get solar: renters, leaseholders in flats, and homeowners whose roof is shaded, north-facing, or simply unsuitable.

What actually changed?

Statutory Instrument SI 2026/848 — The Plugs and Sockets etc. (Safety) Regulations 1994 and Electricity Safety, Quality and Continuity Regulations 2002 (Amendment) Regulations 2026 — was made on 16 July 2026, laid before Parliament on 17 July, and comes into force on 27 August 2026. It allows notified bodies to approve plugs for small solar "microgenerators" that don't need to conform to the usual BS 1363 plug standard, provided the whole kit meets the government's new Plug-in Solar Device Interim Product Specification (v2).

Who's this actually for?

Plug-in solar isn't a replacement for a full rooftop array — it's aimed squarely at people who've never had a solar option at all:

  • Renters, who can't install anything permanent
  • Leaseholders in flats and apartment blocks
  • Homeowners with a shaded, north-facing, or structurally unsuitable roof
  • Anyone who wants to trial solar without a full installation project

☀️ Output is capped at 800W per kit — enough to offset daytime background loads like a fridge, router, lights or laptop charger, but it won't run a whole house. Panel capacity can go up to 2,000W across up to four panels, with the inverter limiting what actually reaches your sockets.

Not every kit on sale is legal — here's what to check

The specification requires the panel, inverter, cable, plug and mounting to be certified together as one complete package — an inverter with its own UKCA mark on its own isn't enough. Compliant kits are verified against the ENA G98 Type Test Register, so it's worth checking a kit is actually on that register before buying, rather than assuming any "plug-in solar" product sold online already qualifies.

The step people are most likely to skip

Every plug-in solar kit still has to be notified to your Distribution Network Operator (DNO) before you connect it, under the standard G98 process. It's a quick online form rather than an approval you have to wait for — but skipping it leaves you non-compliant, and it's an easy step to miss when a kit arrives ready to plug in straight out of the box. The rules are also clear that a kit must go directly into a socket on a healthy circuit — no extension leads or adapters — so it's worth having your circuit checked first, particularly in an older property.

Fits with this year's wider wiring regulation update

This isn't happening in isolation — BS 7671 Amendment 4, published back in April 2026, already introduced the first dedicated rules for small plug-connected PV sources alongside its new battery storage chapter. Between the two, 2026 has been the year plug-in solar went from a grey area to a properly regulated, genuinely legal option.
